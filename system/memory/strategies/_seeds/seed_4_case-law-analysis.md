# Case Law Analysis Strategy

## Steps
1. READ the full opinion — identify the court, date, parties, and procedural posture
2. IDENTIFY the key legal issue(s) the court addressed
3. EXTRACT the holding — what did the court actually decide on each issue?
4. ANALYZE the reasoning — what legal principles, statutes, or precedents did the court rely on?
5. DISTINGUISH — how does this case differ from or align with your client's situation?
6. CHECK subsequent history — has this case been affirmed, reversed, overruled, or distinguished?
7. ASSESS the precedential value — binding vs. persuasive authority in your jurisdiction
8. DOCUMENT in a case brief format: citation, facts, issue, holding, reasoning, relevance to matter
9. INTEGRATE findings into the broader research memo or argument structure

## Negative Constraints
- Do not rely on a case without reading the actual opinion — summaries and headnotes can be misleading
- Do not cite a case as binding authority outside its jurisdiction without noting it is persuasive only
- Do not ignore dissenting opinions — they may signal future shifts in the law
- Do not assume a case is still good law — always check for subsequent treatment

## Notes
- Pay attention to the standard of review — it affects how the holding applies at different procedural stages
- Concurring opinions can provide important insight into the scope of the majority holding
- When multiple issues are addressed, focus your analysis on the issue relevant to your matter
