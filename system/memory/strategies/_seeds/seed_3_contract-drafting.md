# Contract/Agreement Drafting

## Steps
1. Identify the type of agreement and gather all key commercial terms from the client
2. Select an appropriate template or precedent from prior successful engagements
3. Draft the recitals (background/whereas clauses) establishing context and intent
4. Draft the operative provisions — definitions, obligations, rights, conditions
5. Include standard protective clauses: indemnification, limitation of liability, representations & warranties
6. Add boilerplate provisions: governing law, dispute resolution, force majeure, assignment, notices, entire agreement
7. Review for internal consistency — ensure defined terms are used consistently throughout
8. Cross-reference all section references and verify numbering
9. Conduct a final review for ambiguity, gaps, and enforceability risks
10. Prepare a summary of key terms for client review

## Negative Constraints
- Never draft a contract without confirming the governing law — different jurisdictions have different requirements
- Never use undefined terms in operative provisions — every key term must be in the Definitions section
- Never copy clauses from templates without adapting to the specific transaction context
- Never finalize a contract without a cross-reference and numbering check

## Notes
- Plain language drafting improves enforceability and reduces disputes
- Always consider the "what if it goes wrong" scenario for each material provision
- Track all versions and maintain a redline against the original for negotiation clarity
