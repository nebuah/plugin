# Legal Research Workflow

## Steps
1. Clearly define the legal question or issue to be researched
2. Identify the relevant jurisdiction(s) and applicable body of law (federal, state, international)
3. Search for controlling statutes, regulations, and constitutional provisions
4. Search for binding case law from the relevant jurisdiction's highest courts
5. Expand to persuasive authority — sister jurisdictions, lower courts, secondary sources
6. Check that all cited authorities are still good law (not overruled, superseded, or distinguished)
7. Organize findings by issue, separating favorable and unfavorable authorities
8. Synthesize findings into a research memo with citations in proper format

## Negative Constraints
- Never cite a case without verifying it has not been overruled or distinguished on the relevant point
- Never rely solely on headnotes or summaries — always read the full relevant portions of the opinion
- Never present research as exhaustive without documenting the search methodology used
- Never omit unfavorable authority — ethical duty requires acknowledging contrary positions

## Notes
- Start with secondary sources (treatises, law reviews) when entering an unfamiliar area of law
- Use Boolean and natural language searches in combination for thorough coverage
- Document all search queries and databases used for reproducibility
